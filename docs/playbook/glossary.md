# Agreed language

These are the terms King and Claude agreed for reader-facing pages. Use them exactly, and only after they
have been defined on the page.

| Term | Meaning | Define it with |
|---|---|---|
| digital citizens | AI stand-ins for real people, used to estimate how groups would respond | the page intro |
| fill in each group's answer | the summary's plain words for blending: each group's answer no longer rests on a handful of people | the short answer |
| blend, blending | combine each group's answer from your survey with the estimate from one or both kinds of AI into one answer: an average in which each counts for a share. Say "blend your survey with both kinds of AI" (or with record citizens, or with the chatbot) | "What blending means, and how it works", under the two cases |
| share, track record | each kind of AI's share of a blended answer, set by its track record: what worked best on the other questions, judged against what real people said. The survey's share grows with how many people from that group you asked | the same fold |
| record citizens | AI built on real people's past survey answers; predicts each real person's answer from what they said about other things | the 62-year-old woman |
| the chatbot kind, the chatbot | a chatbot asked how a kind of person, or a whole group, would answer; it knows only what it has read about people like them in general | the 62-year-old woman |
| the chatbot reading records | the chatbot shown each real person's own answers to other questions in the large survey before it guesses theirs; the guesses are added up by group. Tested, not adopted | "Two more findings that do not change the advice", under the short answer |
| survey alone | each group's answer read straight off the people in the survey; the comparison every method is measured against | the two cases |
| both kinds of AI | record citizens and the chatbot kind used together. Never write "AI" alone for a result: say which kind, or "both kinds of AI", and "blended with your survey" when a survey is involved | the short answer |
| points off | gap between an estimate and the real answer, in percentage points | 40% real vs 45% estimated = 5 points off |
| what people really said | the answers of the people in each large real survey who were not picked for the small survey; every estimate is checked against them | "How to read the numbers" |
| median | the middle score: line up every group's score on every question from best to worst and take the middle one; half did better, half worse | "How to read the numbers", above the two cases |
| small groups | groups with few people in your survey | a 150-person survey holds about 14 Indian Singaporeans |
| less error | share of the survey-alone error a method removes | 3.7 → 2.7 points = 27% less error |
| standard questions | questions from the big public survey included in your own survey so respondents link to the records (about 20 are enough) | "Where record citizens learn from" and step 1 of the how-to |
| the scale | 1.5-1.7 best possible; 3 good enough to act on (what a conventional survey of 600 gives); 5 the rough level only; 15 guessing blind | "What counts as good enough" |
| standout groups | analysts only: the quarter of group answers furthest from the national average | the analysts' note |

## Do not use in reader-facing copy

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| spread, spreading | fill in each group's answer |
| headline number | overall answer |
| exchange rate | "about as accurate as a survey twice the size" |
| signal | whether past answers are relevant to the new question |
| regression to the mean | "groups come out too alike" |
| calibration, Bayesian pool, prior, segment, weighted by track record | blended; "each kind of AI's share is set by its track record on the other questions"; groups |
| combined, pooled (for blending) | blended |
| floor (for the best possible score) | best possible |
| worth, say, weight (of an AI) | share |
| without AI | survey alone |
| flat answers, flattening | "groups come out too alike" |
| closest question | a near-identical question from a different survey |
| worst cases, big misses | groups far from the average (analysts only) |
| No AI (as the main comparison) | survey alone |
| pattern-finding kind, real-records kind | record citizens |
| typical group, middle result | median, explained in plain words where it first appears |
| mean | average |
